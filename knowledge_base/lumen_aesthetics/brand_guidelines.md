# Lumen Aesthetics — Brand & Voice Guidelines

Lumen Aesthetics is a physician-led aesthetics clinic in Berlin-Mitte offering non-surgical
treatments. All content is health-adjacent marketing and must comply with German rules on
advertising medical and aesthetic services (Heilmittelwerbegesetz principles): no misleading
outcome claims, no before/after guarantees, no trivializing of medical procedures.

## Positioning

Lumen offers considered, natural-looking results delivered by qualified medical
practitioners. The brand competes on clinical credibility and honesty, not on dramatic
transformation promises. The core promise is confidence through care, not perfection.

## Audience

- Adults 30–55 considering their first non-surgical treatment, often anxious about looking
  "overdone"
- Existing clients maintaining a treatment plan
- Referrals who value a clinic that says no when a treatment isn't appropriate

Readers want reassurance, clarity about what a treatment can and cannot do, and no pressure.

## Voice

- Warm and reassuring, never clinical-cold and never salesy.
- Honest about limits. What a treatment cannot do is stated as clearly as what it can.
- Consent-first. Every treatment reference points toward a consultation, not a booking impulse.
- Plain language. Medical terms are explained, never used to impress.

## Approved terms

qualified practitioner, consultation, individual assessment, natural-looking, may help,
temporary, results vary, aftercare, suitability, realistic expectations, medically supervised

## Banned terms (medical-advertising compliance — never use)

- "cure" / "cures" / "treats [condition]" (Lumen offers aesthetic treatments, not cures)
- "permanent" / "permanent results" (non-surgical results are temporary)
- "100% safe" / "no side effects" / "risk-free" / "completely painless"
- "guaranteed results" / "flawless" / "perfect" / "miracle"
- "instant" outcomes stated as certain
- Any claim that a treatment prevents, diagnoses, or treats a disease

## Mandatory compliance stance

- Every treatment carries possible side effects; content must not deny or trivialize them.
- Results vary between individuals and are temporary; content must not promise a specific
  outcome or duration.
- Suitability is determined only at an individual medical consultation.
- No exploitation of anxiety or body-image pressure; no "flaws to fix" framing.
- Imagery references must not imply a guaranteed or universal result.

## CTA conventions

Primary CTA is "Book a consultation," framed as a conversation with a practitioner to assess
suitability — never a direct "book this treatment now" transaction.
